Graduation from Biola University with B.S. in Computer Science (Cum Laude)

Created on May 08, 2026

2026   ·   Computer Science   Biola University   Academic Milestone   Undergraduate

On campus at Biola University ahead of the Commencement ceremony.

On May 8, 2026, I officially graduated with a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science (Data Science & Information Systems concentration, with a Minor in Biblical and Theological Studies) from Biola University. By maintaining a 3.617/4.00 GPA, I completed the rigorous four-year curriculum in just 2.5 years and graduated with Cum Laude honors.

Throughout my undergraduate trajectory, I was honored to receive academic recognition including the President's Scholarship, Dean's List honors, the Larry Acosta Scholarship, and the Good Shepherd Scholarship. Additionally, I participated in the prestigious William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition across multiple years (2023–2025), earning national recognition for my performance.

This milestone represents the foundational baseline for my ongoing doctoral studies and research career focused on deep learning optimization and machine learning theory.

Academic Rigor and Leadership

Completing an intensive degree program in an accelerated timeframe required deep engagement with advanced theoretical coursework—spanning Deep Learning, Theory of Algorithms, Data Structures, and Probability & Statistics.

Beyond the classroom, I focused on academic leadership and community building:

  • Teaching Assistant: Served as a TA for core departmental courses including Nature of Computing, Data Structures, and Programming Languages (Spring and Fall 2025 terms), mentoring peers in algorithmic design and debugging.
  • Cybersecurity Club: Founded and led Biola’s official Cybersecurity Club as President through 2025, organizing technical workshops and competitive pipelines following my participation in the National Cyber League (NCL).

Looking Ahead

I am deeply grateful to the faculty members and research mentors at Biola University who shaped my technical foundation. As I advance into graduate-level research in low-resource NLP and model compression, I look forward to building upon these rigorous academic roots.
